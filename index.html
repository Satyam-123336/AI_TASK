08 Key Insights

Fear Is Profitable — Contrarian Edge Confirmed
Trades during Fear averaged $54.29 vs $34.31 during Neutral. Even Extreme Fear ($34.54) outperforms Neutral. Daily aggregates show Extreme Fear days averaging $49K daily PnL — the highest of any regime. This cohort thrives as contrarians.
Sizing Intelligence — Big in Fear, Small in Greed
Average trade size during Fear ($7,816) is 2.5× larger than during Extreme Greed ($3,112). Top traders instinctively scale up when others panic and scale down near euphoric tops — exactly the professional behaviour expected of sophisticated market participants.
Transition Alpha — Play the Pivot, Not the Regime
The single most profitable trading day configuration is the Fear → Greed transition ($165 avg). Monitoring daily F&G crossovers (especially from sub-40 to above 55) gives a systematic entry signal with strong historical edge in this dataset.
Fee Drag Kills Winners — The Hidden Enemy
Trader #5 (0x513b…) grossed $840K but paid $76K in fees (~9% fee rate), the highest in the cohort. Trader #28 had a 43.9% win rate yet ended negative purely due to fees. Fee optimisation matters more than win rate for HFT-style traders.
🪙
Asset Rotation by Sentiment is Real
HYPE dominates in Fear regimes while @107 (a perpetual index) dominates in Greed/Extreme Greed. This suggests a rational rotation: spot/utility tokens outperform in fear; leveraged indices outperform in bull runs. A systematic rotation model could capture this.
⏰
Time of Day Edge — IST 12 PM is Golden
The 12 PM IST hour (European market open + Asian close overlap) consistently produces the highest average PnL at $131/trade. The 7 AM–12 PM window captures 5 of the top 6 hours by average PnL — aligning with Asian session continuation and European morning momentum.
Top Traders Trade Less Frequently, Earn More
Trader #2 (0x0833…) made $1.59M with only 3,818 trades — a high avg PnL of $417/trade. Trader #4 (0xbee1…) needed 40,184 trades for $822K ($20/trade). Quality > quantity: fewer, higher-conviction trades produce superior risk-adjusted returns.
Bottom Traders Over-Trade in Fear Markets
Bottom-5 traders had 25% exposure to Extreme Fear vs only 11.7% for top-5 traders. They didn't avoid fear — they traded it poorly. This suggests lack of risk management, wrong directional bias, or overleveraging during volatile periods — not avoiding fear, but failing to capitalise on it.

10 Conclusion

This analysis of 211,224 trades across 32 Hyperliquid traders reveals a cohort with a pronounced contrarian edge: they perform best when market sentiment is fearful, deploy larger capital when others panic, and profit most from sentiment regime transitions — especially when fear converts to greed.

The weakest signal is during Neutral conditions (F&G 45–55), where traders face directionless markets and the only loss-making transition (Neutral → Greed, −$15.73) occurs. Avoiding over-trading in this zone and respecting fee drag are the two highest-ROI operational improvements available to this group.

Strategically, combining the F&G transition signal + intraday timing filter + asset rotation model could systematically enhance performance by an estimated 20–40% on risk-adjusted basis, based on the spreads observed between best and worst configurations in this dataset.
